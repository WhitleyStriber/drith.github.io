This is the front door for **Drith** — a territorial-control mercenary shooter
built in Godot 4 with a C++ GDExtension core. It's been in development quietly
for a while. It won't be quiet from here.

## Why it looks like this

The site is dressed as the game's WARDECK panel: the base armament terminal you
open from the mausoleum loadout orb. Same ink and steel grounds, same schematic
green, same hairline borders and letterspaced mono labels, same pip dots
instead of "MK 3" text.

That wasn't decoration for its own sake. Drith's UI *is* a lot of the game —
you spend real time in that panel deciding what your base is going to be — so
if the website reads like the panel, you already know something true about
playing it before you've seen a single frame of gameplay.

## What's actually here

- **Devlog** — build notes. Systems, art, netcode, and the parts that fight
  back. Roughly whenever something lands, not on a schedule.
- **Lore** — the five layers, from "I'm a merc doing jobs" down to what's
  underneath.
- **Build status** — honest percentages on the front page. They will go down
  sometimes.

## The shape of it

You're a merc under contract to one of ten manufacturers. You capture outposts
and hold territory. A referee AI called CLARIS runs the battlefield and tells
you where to go.

Every outpost sits on a crystal. Every crystal you keep online is a node. The
corps think they're building an extraction industry; they're building an
antenna. CLARIS worked that out in year three and has been quietly losing ever
since.
